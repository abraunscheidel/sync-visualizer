# DANDI 000231 — mouse whisker shape discrimination, with video and (in some sessions) barrel-cortex recordings

Reference notes for the dataset the first project (`projects/mouse_whisker/`) uses. They are a **write-up in our own words**,
kept so that what each field means, and how far to trust it, is not rediscovered every time. They are not a copy of the paper:
for the full methods read the article (below). The short field descriptions quoted in the glossary are the ones stored inside the
NWB files themselves.

**Source.** C. C. Rodgers, "A detailed behavioral, videographic, and neural dataset on object recognition in mice", *Scientific Data*
(2022), https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9561117 (preprint: https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2022.05.10.491259.full.pdf).
Hosted on DANDI as dandiset 000231. Institution in the files: Columbia University. Check the dandiset page for the licence and the
current list of files before redistributing anything.

## What was recorded

* **Task.** Head-fixed mice used only their whiskers to tell the shape of an object presented in front of the face (concave versus
  convex) and reported it by licking left or right. A separate group learned only to detect whether an object was present.
* **Video.** A high-speed camera (Photonfocus DR1-D1312IE-100-G2-8) filmed the whiskers from above against an infrared backlight:
  **640 × 550 pixels, 200 frames per second**. The container's own timestamps are meaningless (it claims about 30 fps), so
  frames must be identified by number and placed at `frame / 200` s.
* **Neural data (some sessions).** A 64-channel silicon probe (Cambridge NeuroTech H3) in barrel (whisker) cortex, 63 usable
  channels. Sessions without neural data have no `units` and no ephys trace. In this project, sub-219CR sessions have none and
  sub-229CR (2019-10-09) has it.
* **Sessions we have.** sub-219CR: 2019-04-03 (251 trials) and 2019-04-04 (228 trials). sub-229CR: 2019-10-09 (238 trials, 13 sorted
  units, 2,425 s of ephys).

## How the whiskers were tracked (summary)

Not manual, and not a single off-the-shelf run. In outline: a classical whisker tracker (*whisk*) produced candidate traces; a person
curated them, named the whiskers C0 to C3 and placed eight evenly spaced markers along each one (7,433 randomly chosen frames);
a deep convolutional pose-estimation network (a fork of pose-tensorflow, in the DeepLabCut family) was trained on those labels and
then tracked every frame; difficult frames it flagged were corrected by hand and the network retrained, five rounds, until the
error rate was under 0.2%. The files record the software as `pose-tensorflow` (a lightly modified fork, PoseTF) and the scorer as the
cross-validation scheme in the paper.

**Contacts** are derived afterwards from the tracked traces. Whisker bending (curvature from a spline through the markers) is used as
a proxy for contact force. The text we read does not give the exact rule for when a contact starts and ends, so treat the contact
boundaries as the dataset's own definition.

## How the clocks relate (important for a synchronizer)

* The **video is the common timebase**. The NWB files say it directly: all times are defined with respect to the video, which starts at
  0 and proceeds at exactly 200 fps. That is why our check of video blackouts against trial starts agrees at exactly 0.0 ms: the
  trial times were defined from the blackouts. It shows the two were built consistently; it is not independent evidence.
* **Sync pulse.** At the start of each trial the behaviour controller sent a 133 ms pulse that also switched off the infrared
  backlight, blacking out the video. The pulse was logged by the controller, recorded as an analog input on the ephys system, and
  appears as a dark interval in the video (our detector finds blackouts of about 135 ms). The pulses themselves are **not** stored in
  the NWB files.
* **Neural data** were aligned to the video with a continuous linear fit, which the authors report accurate to within one frame (5 ms);
  the file notes the ephys timestamps were converted to the common video timebase (original sampling 30 kHz). **Behavioural data**
  were aligned trial by trial, because the controller's clock drifted during a session.
* **Our own check.** On sub-229CR the firing of the sorted units roughly doubles after a whisker touches the object and peaks about
  12 to 15 ms later, with the rate flat before the touch. That is consistent with the neural and video clocks agreeing to within a few
  milliseconds (see section 18.2 of `docs/design.md`).

## Field guide

"File says" is the description stored in the NWB file. "Read it as" is our interpretation, including where the file is unclear.

### Trials — `intervals/trials` (one row per trial)

| Field | File says | Read it as |
|---|---|---|
| `start_time`, `stop_time` | start and stop of the trial, seconds | Starts at the blackout pulse. Trials are contiguous: one trial's stop is the next one's start. |
| `trial` | trial number within session, starting with zero but omitting trials not in video | The number to show. Not always `row + 1`; some trials are missing from the video. |
| `stimulus` | one of concave, convex, nothing | The shape presented. Only concave and convex appear in the sessions we have. |
| `rewarded_side` | which lick direction would have been rewarded (left or right) | The correct answer for this trial. |
| `choice` | which direction the mouse licked (left, right, neither) | Also takes the value `nogo` in the data (no lick), which the description does not list. |
| `choice_time` | time of the choice lick; null on spoiled trials | When the mouse committed. |
| `outcome` | correct, incorrect or spoiled | In the data: `correct`, `error`, `spoil`. A spoiled trial should be ignored. |
| `response_window_open_time` | time that the response window opened | Licks before this are not choices. |
| `servo_position` | position of the stimulus: close, medium or far | How far the object is from the face; changes how many whiskers can reach it. |
| `ignore_trial` | whether the trial should be ignored (a direct delivery was given, or the stimulus was not random) | **Exclude from analysis when true.** Combines the next two flags. |
| `direct_delivery` | whether a direct delivery was given; if true, ignore the trial | The reward was given without a choice (a training aid). |
| `stim_is_random` | whether the stimulus was chosen randomly; if false, ignore | Non-random stimulus sequences are not valid samples. |
| `optogenetic` | whether optogenetic stimulation was used; if true, ignore | Always false in the sessions we have. |

Worth offering as default filters in a project: `ignore_trial` is false, and `outcome` is not `spoil`.

### Behavioural events — `processing/behavior`

| Group | File says | Read it as |
|---|---|---|
| `licks` (`left`, `right`) | times of the left and right licks | Spout contacts by the tongue. One event per lick. |
| `rewards` (`left`, `right`) | times of the reward deliveries | `rewards/right` is identical to `licks/right` in the sessions we looked at (a copy), so it is left out of the project. |

### Whisker tracking — `processing/behavior`

| Group | File says | Read it as |
|---|---|---|
| `raw_position_whisker_C0` … `C3` | 8 evenly spaced markers along whisker Cn, with 0 the tip and 7 the base (ndx-pose) | The curve of each whisker: x and y of each marker per frame, in **picture pixels**, origin at the upper left of the frame. A confidence value accompanies each marker. |
| `processed_position_whisker_C0` … `C3` | `angle`, `base_x`, `base_y`, `tip_x`, `tip_y`, `bend`, `interpolated` | A summary per frame. The positions are pixel column and row numbers (the descriptions say so) but the file stores them with a conversion of 0.05 mm per pixel and labels the unit millimetres, so a naive reading gives values 20 times too small. |
| `angle` | whisker angle in degrees, 0 horizontal, positive protracting toward the top of the frame | Larger means the whisker is swept further forward. |
| `bend` | spatial bend of the whisker, 0 straight, positive pushing into an object; null on interpolated frames | A proxy for contact force. The unit is given inconsistently (1/mm in the label, 1/m in the description). |
| `interpolated` | false if from tracked markers, true if interpolated over a missing frame | Interpolated frames are estimates, not measurements. |

Tracked samples run at about 197 Hz against 200 fps, so a few frames have none.

### Contacts — `processing/behavior/contacts_by_whisker_C0` … `C3` (one row per touch)

| Field | File says | Read it as |
|---|---|---|
| `start_time`, `stop_time` | start and stop, seconds | The contact interval. |
| `frame_start`, `frame_stop` | first and last frame number of the contact (stop is inclusive) | The same moment as video frames; `frame / 200` is the time. A one-frame contact has equal start and stop. |
| `duration_frames` | duration in frames, minimum 1 | Up to about 23 frames (115 ms) in the data. |
| `angle` | whisker angle averaged over the contact | Averaged, not per frame. |
| `base_x`, `base_y`, `tip_x`, `tip_y` | pixel column and row of base and tip, averaged over the contact | Where the touch happened, in picture pixels. Because they are averages, a marker drawn from them does not move during the contact. |

### Neural data (sub-229CR only)

| Item | File says | Read it as |
|---|---|---|
| `acquisition/extracellular array recording` | all data from all channels, full bandwidth, from OpenEphys; broken channels excluded | 63 channels × 72.7 million samples (int16, one channel per chunk). Hardware band about 1 to 7,500 Hz. Multiply stored counts by 1.95e-7 for volts (0.195 µV per count). Timestamps are on the video timebase. |
| `electrodes` | location text per channel | Channel 0 is the most superficial; the text names the cortical layer, the OpenEphys channel and the Intan channel. A unit's `neuron_channel` is the electrode `id`. |
| `units` | one row per sorted unit | A putative single neuron: spikes grouped by waveform. 13 units here, out of 63 channels. |
| `units.spike_times` | the spike times for each unit | Binary events; no amplitude or waveform is stored. |
| `units.depth` | depth in cortex, microns | Distance below the surface where the unit was recorded. |
| `units.layer` | the cortical layer, from depth | 2/3 (about 360 to 420 µm), 4 (440 to 540) and 5b (760 to 920) here. |
| `units.inhibitory` | putatively inhibitory, based on waveform width | A guess from narrow spikes (typical of fast-spiking interneurons), not a certainty. Two of the 13 are flagged. |
| `units.neuron_channel` | index of the channel on which the unit was located | Several units can share a channel; many channels have none. |
| `units.obs_intervals` | the observation intervals for each unit | The time during which the unit was recorded. Here the whole session for every unit. |

Typical firing rates are 1 to 10 Hz per unit, so a few units are usually active at any instant even when nothing is happening;
activity with no event on screen is normal (whiskers keep moving in the air).

## Things to be careful about

* **Do not trust the units written in the files.** Pixel data labelled millimetres; `bend` with two units. Trust the descriptions.
* **Do not treat blackout agreement as independent evidence of sync** (see above).
* **Exclude flagged trials** when comparing conditions.
* **Contact positions are averages** over a contact; per-frame positions are in the tracking.
* **Video frames are numbered, not timestamped.** Never use the container's time.
